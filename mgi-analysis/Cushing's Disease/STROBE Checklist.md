## STROBE Checklist for Case–Control Studies (Main Items)

| Item No. | Recommendation |
|----------|----------------|
| **Title and Abstract** ||
| 1 | Indicate the study’s design with a commonly used term in the title or abstract. Provide an informative and balanced summary of what was done and what was found. |
| **Introduction** ||
| 2 | Explain the scientific background and rationale. |
| 3 | State specific objectives, including prespecified hypotheses. |
| **Methods** ||
| 4 | Present key elements of study design early in the paper. |
| 5 | Describe the setting, locations, and relevant dates (recruitment, exposure, follow-up, data collection). |
| 6 | Give eligibility criteria for cases and controls and the sources/methods of case ascertainment and control selection. |
| 7 | Clearly define all exposures, outcomes, predictors, confounders, and effect modifiers. Give diagnostic criteria for cases. |
| 8 | For matched studies, specify matching criteria and the number of controls per case. |
| 9 | Describe methods used to address potential sources of bias. |
| 10 | Explain how the study size was arrived at. |
| 11 | Explain how quantitative variables were handled (grouping, transformations). |
| 12 | Describe all statistical methods, including:  
   - Methods for controlling confounding  
   - Methods for examining subgroups and interactions  
   - How matching of cases and controls was addressed  
   - Handling of missing data  
   - Any sensitivity analyses |
| **Results** ||
| 13 | Report numbers of individuals at each stage of the study (consider a flow diagram). |
| 14 | Give characteristics of study participants (demographic, clinical, social) and information on exposures/confounders. Indicate missing data for each variable. |
| 15 | Give numbers in each exposure category or summary measures of exposure. |
| 16 | Give unadjusted estimates and, if applicable, confounder-adjusted estimates and their precision (e.g., 95% CI). Report category boundaries when continuous variables are categorized. Consider translating relative risks into absolute risks for a meaningful time period. |
| **Discussion** ||
| 17 | Summarize key results with reference to study objectives. |
| 18 | Discuss limitations, considering potential sources of bias or imprecision. |
| 19 | Give a cautious overall interpretation considering objectives, limitations, multiplicity of analyses, results from similar studies, and other evidence. |
| 20 | Discuss the generalizability (external validity) of the study results. |
| **Other Information** ||
| 21 | Give the source of funding and the role of the funders. |
| 22 | State ethical approval and consent (if applicable). |
